# Growth Metrics That Actually Matter — Sample Lenny Podcast Transcript Fixture

**NOTE: This is a clearly labeled sample fixture for demo/RAG testing.**

Lenny Rachitsky: Marcus, everyone tracks everything. What metrics do you wish PMs would stop obsessing over?

Marcus Chen: Vanity top-of-funnel numbers. Raw signups without activation rate context create false confidence. I'd rather see fewer signups with higher week-one retention than a spike from a campaign that attracts tourists.

Lenny: What should early-stage teams focus on?

Marcus: Pick one north star tied to value delivery. For marketplace companies, it's often completed transactions per active user. For SaaS, it's weekly active teams performing the core action. Support that with input metrics you can move in two-week cycles: activation rate, invite rate, integration connect rate — whatever matches your model.

Lenny: How do you think about pricing experiments in growth?

Marcus: Price tests belong after you understand value, not before. Interview customers on willingness to pay only after they've experienced the product. Value-based pricing starts with quantifying the outcome you deliver — time saved, revenue gained, risk reduced — not with competitor spreadsheets alone.

Lenny: Retention versus acquisition?

Marcus: Retention is the growth metric with the highest leverage once you have product-market fit signal. A 5% improvement in month-three retention often beats a 20% increase in paid acquisition because compounding cohorts reduce blended CAC over time. Before PMF, you might weight acquisition higher to learn faster — but be explicit about the phase you're in.

Lenny: What role does qualitative research play?

Marcus: Metrics tell you what moved; interviews tell you why. Every quarterly planning cycle should include reviewing support tickets, sales call notes, and five user interviews per PM. Ground decisions in both charts and quotes.

Lenny: Closing thoughts?

Marcus: If your dashboard can't explain a retention dip within 48 hours, you have an instrumentation problem, not a growth problem. Fix visibility first, then optimize loops.
